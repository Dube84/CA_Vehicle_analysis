About Me

I am a high school student with a strong interest in cars and the automotive market. Outside of school, I follow industry news around new vehicle releases, electric vehicles, and how consumer preferences are shifting. This project gave me the chance to combine that personal interest with data analysis to answer a question I genuinely care about: how has the car market actually changed over the last 40 years?

Project Goals

The objective behind this project was to analyse real-world vehicle data to identify meaningful trends in the kinds of cars people drive. Specifically, I wanted to explore how fuel economy, engine size, body style popularity, and fuel type have changed over time.

I used the dataset titled "Vehicle Fuel Economy," which includes statistics from every vehicle configuration registered in California from 1984–2015. It covers fuel type, engine displacement, vehicle class, combined MPG, and more. I processed and visualised this data using Python, pandas, Matplotlib, and Claude as an AI assistant throughout development.

Key Findings

After analysing the full dataset, four major trends stood out clearly across the 30-year period.

↑ Rising

Fuel Economy (MPG)

Average combined MPG has risen steadily, driven by improvements in engine efficiency, the growth of hybrids, and tighter emissions regulations over the decades.

↑ Rising

SUV Market Share

Sport Utility Vehicles have grown from a niche segment to the dominant body style, gradually overtaking sedans and wagons as the most popular vehicle choice.

↓ Declining

Average Engine Size

Mean engine displacement has trended downward as turbocharged small-displacement engines and electrified powertrains replaced large naturally-aspirated motors.

↓ Declining

Sedan Market Share

Cars and sedans have been steadily phased out of the mainstream market, losing ground to SUVs and crossovers throughout the dataset's time range.

Predictions

Based on the trends observed in the data, here is what I expect to see over the next four years:

  • MPG will keep climbing — The push for better fuel efficiency, combined with the growing adoption of electric and hybrid vehicles, will continue to lift the average combined MPG figure year on year.
  • Engine sizes will keep shrinking — As EVs and plug-in hybrids take a larger slice of new vehicle sales, average engine displacement will fall further. Even among combustion vehicles, downsized turbocharged engines are now the norm.
  • SUVs will dominate even more — Consumer preference has clearly shifted toward SUVs and crossovers. I expect this trend to accelerate, with SUVs accounting for an ever-larger share of new vehicle configurations.
  • Sedans will continue to be phased out — Several major manufacturers have already reduced or eliminated their sedan line-ups. This trend is unlikely to reverse in the near term.

Dataset

The dataset ({{ dataset_name }}) is a CSV file containing one row per vehicle configuration entry. Key columns used in this project include:

  • year — Model year of the vehicle configuration.
  • make / model — Manufacturer and model name.
  • VClass — EPA vehicle class label, used to derive a simplified body style category.
  • comb08 — Combined fuel economy rating in MPG (or MPGe for electric vehicles).
  • displ — Engine displacement in litres.
  • fuelType1 — Primary fuel type, used to identify diesel vehicles.

Each row represents a distinct vehicle configuration (make, model, trim, and powertrain combination), not a registration count or sales figure. Aggregate statistics therefore reflect the diversity of configurations available in each model year rather than fleet volumes.

Methodology

The dashboard builds four trend charts and one summary table:

  • Fuel Economy Over Time — Annual mean of comb08 (rows with missing values are excluded).
  • Engine Size Over Time — Annual mean of displ, restricted to rows where displacement is greater than zero.
  • Body Style Mix — VClass values are mapped to six simplified categories (Car, SUV, Pickup Truck, Van, Wagon, Special Purpose). The top five categories by total count are shown as stacked area shares per year.
  • Engine Displacement by Body Style — Annual mean displacement for each of the top five body style categories, plotted as separate trend lines.
  • Diesel Share Over Time — Percentage of configurations whose fuelType1 contains the word "diesel" (case-insensitive), calculated per model year.
  • Five-Year Snapshots — Aggregated statistics (average MPG, average engine size, leading body style, and row count) for every fifth model year in the dataset.

Data cleaning is performed at load time: numeric columns are coerced with pd.to_numeric(errors="coerce") so that non-numeric entries become NaN and are automatically excluded from aggregations.
